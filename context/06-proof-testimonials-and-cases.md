## 4. Public case stories

The business has published dozens of real case narratives since 2012. These are its strongest content asset — but they contain real names, faces, places, and sensitive details. **Consent and anonymisation are mandatory for reuse.**

Below are the documented stories, with sources. Use them as **research material**, not as ready-to-publish copy.

### 4.1 Family reunions

**Juan Alberto Sosa López (68) — two siblings reunited after 34 years (2012)**
His sister, Edilberta Alicia Sosa López (69), from Puno, asked the agency to find her brother, whom she had not seen in 34 years. He had left at 16 for Lima, married, and lived in El Agustino. Both were elderly and ill. The agency contacted his wife, who facilitated a phone reunion. The wife said: "Qué regalo de Navidad más hermoso que recibe mi esposo. Muchas gracias."
*Source: old site, "Personas ubicadas", 19 Dec 2012.*

**Melchor Martínez Tomateo (76) — a daughter finds her father after 30 years (2012)**
Elizabeth Lima, a young entrepreneur in Tacna, had only a name and no maternal surname. RENIEC and other agencies had failed. The agency identified him, found him in Villa María del Triunfo, and took Elizabeth to his home. A half-sister, Azucena, recognized her after finding an old letter. The whole family celebrated at a restaurant.
*Source: old site, "Personas ubicadas", 17 Oct 2012.*

**Lucero Cier Peña — a father finds his daughter in Chile (2012)**
Wilfredo Cier Pino asked on 30 Sep 2012; the last information was from Lima in 1992. The agency located her in Chile. Her thank-you message: "Lucero… esa parte de mí que faltaba… al fin estará completa… te quiero papá. Muchas gracias don Adolfo Funes… no sabe la felicidad que ha traído a mi vida."
*Source: old site, "Personas ubicadas", 5 Oct 2012.*

**Regina Balboa Benites — a son finds his mother after 39 years (2012)**

## 5. What the proof tells us about the business

### Patterns
- **The core case is an adult child searching for a parent**, often after 20–40 years.
- **The second most common is siblings** separated by migration or family breakup.
- **Poverty and internal migration** run through many stories: Chota, Cajamarca, Apurímac, Cusco, Loreto, Puno.
- **The agency acts as a mediator**, not just a locator: it calls the found person, speaks to their current family, prepares the reunion.
- **Some cases end in death.** The business handles this with public condolences — a sign of emotional maturity that the new site should preserve.
- **The public bulletins** show a community-service dimension: sharing a missing-person notice even when it is not a paying case.
- **International cases** (Chile, Colombia, Ecuador) are real, not just marketing.

### What is missing
- No case study includes the client's consent language.
- No dates of birth/current ages are verified.
- No outcomes are independently verified beyond the business's own posts.
- No photos of reunions are in the repo (only stock).
- No video testimonials, no before/after (ethically tricky), no press coverage.

## 6. How to use this material in the redesign

### Recommended
- Build a **"Historias"** section with 6–10 consented stories, each with a clear structure: the separation, the search, the turning point, the reunion, a quote.
- Use **first names or initials + year + country** when full identity is sensitive.
- Add a **content note**: "Historias compartidas con autorización. Algunos nombres se han cambiado para proteger la privacidad."
- Include at least one **difficult outcome** (death or refusal) to show honesty.
- Use **real photography** of reunions if consent exists; otherwise use abstract/archival imagery, not stock families.
- Add **reviews** with source and date.
- Add a **"Verifica nuestra organización"** block: address, page creation date, reviews, team.

### Avoid
- Using "Saul Goodman", "John Larson" or "Jena Karlis".
- Stock portraits labeled as clients.
- "100% efectividad".
- Publishing a located person's full name, face, address, or workplace without consent.
- Publishing children's identities.
- Sensationalising disappearances or conflict.
- Promising a happy ending.
